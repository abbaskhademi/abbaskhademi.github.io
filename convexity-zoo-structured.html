Definitions & main properties

Each numbered entry is based on the matching statement in the paper. Notes summarize the definition or the role of the result; see the original paper for all hypotheses and proofs.

Definition

8.1 · Log-Convex Function

For a positive function, log-convexity is convexity of the logarithm.

f(λx+(1−λ)y) ≤ f(x)^λ f(y)^(1−λ).

Source: Khademi (2026), Definition 8.1, p. 4246 ↗

Property

8.2 · Log-Transform Convexity

Log-convexity of a positive f is equivalent to convexity of log f.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.2, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.3 · Exponential Representation

A positive log-convex function can be represented as eʰ for a convex h.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.3, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.4 · Power Convexity

Examines log-convexity through power transforms; check the exact quantifiers in the paper.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.4, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.5 · Reciprocal Log-Concavity

Log-convexity of f corresponds to log-concavity of its reciprocal.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.5, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.6 · Generalized Jensen Inequality

A finite weighted geometric-mean form extends Jensen’s inequality.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.6, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.7 · Gradient Ratio Monotonicity

For differentiable positive functions, monotonicity of ∇f/f characterizes log-convexity.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.7, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.8 · Log-Gradient Inequality

Log-convexity has two-sided inequalities for logarithmic differences and gradient ratios.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.8, p. 4247 ↗

Property

8.9 · Modified Hessian Condition

For twice differentiable f>0, the modified Hessian f∇²f−∇f∇fᵀ is positive semidefinite.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.9, p. 4248 ↗

Property

8.10 · Log-Convexity Implies convexity

Positive log-convex functions are convex.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.10, p. 4248 ↗

Property

8.11 · Concavity Implies Log-Convexity

Positive concave functions are log-concave.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.11, p. 4248 ↗

Definition

8.12 · Exponentially Convex Function

Exponential convexity means that the exponential transformation of f is convex.

e^(f(λx+(1−λ)y)) ≤ λe^(f(x))+(1−λ)e^(f(y)).

Source: Khademi (2026), Definition 8.12, p. 4248 ↗

Property

8.13 · Logarithmic Characterization

Exponential convexity is characterized by a logarithm of a weighted exponential average.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.13, p. 4248 ↗

Property

8.14 · Relationship to r-Convexity

Exponential convexity coincides with r-convexity at r=1.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.14, p. 4248 ↗

Property

8.15 · Exponential Convexity Implies Convexity

Compares exponential convexity to ordinary convexity; check this implication separately for your applications.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.15, p. 4248 ↗

Definition

8.16 · DC Function

A DC function can be written as the difference of two convex functions.

f(x)=g(x)−h(x), with g and h convex.

Source: Khademi (2026), Definition 8.16, p. 4249 ↗

Property

8.18 · Universality of DC Functions

Every twice continuously differentiable function on a compact convex domain admits a DC decomposition.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.18, p. 4249 ↗

Property

8.19 · Algebraic Closure

DC functions possess useful algebraic closure properties.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.19, p. 4249 ↗

Property

8.20 · Subdifferential Characterization

The Clarke subdifferential of a DC difference is bounded by the corresponding convex subdifferentials.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.20, p. 4249 ↗

Property

8.21 · DC Structure of Other Function Classes

Describes how other structured classes fit the DC framework.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.21, p. 4249 ↗

Property

8.22 · DC Decomposition via Quadratic Regularization

Quadratic regularization yields an explicit DC decomposition under a Hessian lower bound.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.22, p. 4250 ↗

Definition

8.24 · Abstract Convex Function

Abstract convexity replaces affine support functions by a chosen family H of elementary functions.

f(x)=sup{h(x): h∈G}, for some G⊆H.

Source: Khademi (2026), Definition 8.24, p. 4250 ↗

Property

8.25 · Classical Convexity as a Special Case

Classical convexity is recovered from abstract convexity with affine elementary functions.

Source: Khademi (2026), Property 8.25, p. 4254 ↗
